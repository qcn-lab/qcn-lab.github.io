## Overview

We aim to explore how artificial intelligence can become a more general and active tool for scientific discovery in physics. Beyond conventional applications such as prediction and data fitting, we are interested in using AI to discover new algorithms, identify physical strategies, extract hidden information from experimental data, and eventually participate more deeply in the scientific workflow.

Our previous work has explored several complementary forms of AI-driven discovery. Program synthesis has been used to autonomously discover generalizable algorithms for photonic unitary decomposition and automated physical design, demonstrating that AI can uncover interpretable strategies beyond manually constructed schemes. Deep learning has also been integrated with physical models to recover high-resolution information from broadened and noisy nuclear spectroscopy measurements, showing how AI can enhance the information obtained from real experiments. In quantum control, neural networks have been used to directly search physical control spaces and discover nonadiabatic quantum-storage strategies beyond conventional EIT and Raman protocols.

Building on these efforts, we are further developing AI agents that can interact with scientific software and experimental hardware. Our long-term goal is to connect reasoning, computation, data analysis, and physical experiments within a unified framework, enabling more autonomous and closed-loop scientific exploration.
