Light–matter interactions lie at the heart of quantum optics, providing powerful tools for manipulating quantum states, engineering radiative processes, and enhancing the sensitivity of quantum measurements. We aim to extend these concepts into the X-ray regime, where resonant interactions between X-ray photons and atomic nuclei offer a unique platform for exploring quantum dynamics with exceptionally narrow resonances and high spectral precision.

Toward this goal, our research in nuclear quantum optics focuses on three main directions. First, we investigate thin-film X-ray photonic structures containing resonant Mössbauer nuclei, with an emphasis on optimizing light–matter interactions, enhancing sensing performance, and exploring topological phenomena. Second, we study collective and many-body effects in nuclear ensembles under strong X-ray excitation, including superradiant bursts and superradiant lasing. Third, we explore the emerging ²²⁹Th platform, developing new excitation schemes and refining theoretical models, including nuclear excitation by electron capture (NEEC) and the treatment of inhomogeneous broadening.

Through these efforts, we seek to uncover new mechanisms for controlling nuclear quantum dynamics and establish new platforms for non-Hermitian quantum optics, nuclear quantum technologies, and enhanced sensing.
